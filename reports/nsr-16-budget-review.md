## Finding

NSR-16 is a high-confidence Volume II candidate. It is not just a budget file:
it directly asks the administration to review Function 150 programs, program
objectives, management and coordination arrangements, foreign-assistance
legislation, congressional micromanagement, Executive Branch flexibility, and
public support for international affairs programs.

The same online scan also contains a substantial Chief-of-Mission/overseas
staffing package, including NSDD 38 implementation material. That makes this
file relevant to both the budget-management and overseas-staffing lanes.

## Core NSR-16 Content

The signed NSR is dated June 7, 1989. It was addressed to the Vice President,
the Secretaries of State, Treasury, Defense, and Agriculture, OMB, the Assistant
to the President for National Security Affairs, the Assistant to the President
for Economic and Domestic Policy, JCS, AID, and USIA.

The review covers international affairs programs funded through Function 150,
including:

- Bilateral security, economic, and development assistance.
- Multilateral assistance.
- International communications, including USIA.
- Counter-narcotics assistance.
- Refugee assistance.
- Contributions to international organizations.
- Related activities.

The directive orders NSC and OMB to coordinate the review and complete it by
July 21, 1989, to guide the FY 1991 budget.

## Questions Asked by the NSR

The document asks for:

- A current allocation map of international affairs programs.
- A test of whether objectives are adequately defined.
- A review of whether programs are tied to foreign-policy interests.
- Resource implications from regional policy reviews.
- Measures of program effectiveness.
- Current management and coordination arrangements, including overhead.
- The effect of the Hamilton/Fascell reform effort.
- Ways to address requirements/resource shortfalls, including allied
  coordination.
- Congressional strategy for the international affairs budget.
- Policy options for Function 150.
- Recommended shifts in program and regional priorities.
- Changes to evaluation methods.
- Recommendations for legislative change, reduced micromanagement, and greater
  Executive Branch flexibility.
- Structural or management changes to improve coordination across programs.

## Embedded Overseas Staffing Material

The scan includes `Chief of Mission Authority and Overseas Staffing`, an
11-page report on COM authority, the Foreign Service Act, NSDD 38, the 1986
Diplomatic Security and Antiterrorism Act, non-Executive Branch personnel, DEA
and AID/RIG restrictions, and the Special Embassy Program.

Selection value:

- It states that authority over size, composition, and mandate of mission
  staffing rests with the Chief of Mission and Secretary of State.
- It frames overseas staffing reduction as a policy, economy, and security goal.
- It identifies State Management Policy (M/MP) as the custodian of COM
  authority on overseas staffing.
- It describes COM-initiated and agency-initiated staffing-change procedures.
- It makes NSDD 38 a practical mechanism for interagency personnel control at
  posts.
- It includes the 1982 NSDD 38 and guidelines, plus a 1986 all-post Secretary
  message on staffing-request procedure.
