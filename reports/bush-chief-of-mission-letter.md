# President Bush Chief-of-Mission Letter

Checked: 2026-05-22

Volume: *Foreign Relations of the United States, 1989-1992, Volume II,
Organization and Management of Foreign Policy*

## Finding

The final-version candidate is President Bush's July 12, 1990 White House letter
of instruction to all U.S. Chiefs of Mission, not the September 14, 1989
Armacost/Japan version in the NARA transition scan.

This is a strong Volume II inclusion target. It defines the ambassador as the
President's personal representative, sets the Chief of Mission's authority over
executive branch personnel abroad, routes policy guidance and reporting through
the Secretary of State, addresses overseas staffing control, and assigns direct
personal responsibility for mission security.

## Version Notes

The September 14, 1989 NARA version and the July 12, 1990 published version are
closely related, but not identical. The September 1989 letter is addressed to
Ambassador Michael Armacost and uses Japan-specific phrasing. The July 1990
version is generic, addressed to "Mr./Madam Ambassador," and was distributed to
all Chiefs of Mission.

Substantive differences visible in the final published version include:

- Broader post-Cold War framing and the "partner" formulation.
- More explicit handling of State Department channels for policy proposals and
  implementation.
- A detailed overseas staffing-control section tying staff levels to national
  goals, security, and economy.
- Explicit Chief-of-Mission responsibility for mission security,
  counterintelligence, and counterterrorism support.
- Ethical-conduct and equal-employment expectations framed as presidential
  instructions.

## Inclusion Recommendation

Include the July 12, 1990 final all-chief-of-mission letter if a signed or
circulated official copy can be located. It is directly on point for Volume II:
field command authority, interagency management overseas, State's channel role,
overseas staffing control, security management, EEO, and ethics.

Do not rely on the September 14, 1989 Armacost version as the final text unless
no July 1990 archival copy can be located. If the September version is selected
or cited, flag it as a predecessor or country-specific variant.
