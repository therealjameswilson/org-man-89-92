# Provisional Volume Outline

Checked: 2026-05-24

Volume: *Foreign Relations of the United States, 1989-1992, Volume II,
Organization and Management of Foreign Policy*

## Compiler Premise

The volume should not become a miscellaneous process anthology. Its center of
gravity should be how the Bush administration organized, funded, staffed, and
managed foreign policy across State, the NSC, the White House, posts overseas,
Congress, and implementing agencies.

## Proposed Architecture

### 1. Transition and Inherited Machinery

Purpose: establish the institutional starting point in January 1989.

Candidate sources:

- Reagan-Bush transition briefing book on NSC organization and administration,
  NAID 470760855.
- NSC Administrative Manual, NAID 470760856, as source-note support.
- NSC Staff Manual / Secretariat Procedure Manual / NSC Procedural Manual
  binders if a process chapter requires fuller documentation.
- Baker confirmation statement, CP 1146.

Selection logic: use one strong opening document plus source-note context. Avoid
spending document slots on routine manuals unless they show decision machinery.

### 2. State, NSC, and White House Management Relationships

Purpose: show how foreign-policy coordination was supposed to run among the
Secretary of State, National Security Advisor, White House staff, and agencies.

Candidate sources:

- NSC transition organization/process material.
- Baker, Eagleburger, Kimmitt, Zoellick, and S/S files.
- White House Cabinet Affairs, Policy Development, Sununu, and Chief of Staff
  files.

Selection logic: prioritize internal memoranda over public descriptions. This
chapter is still archive-dependent.

### 3. Function 150, Budget, and Program Review

Purpose: make budget/resource management a central organizing theme.

Candidate sources:

- NSR-16, June 7, 1989, and any response/options/follow-on decision records.
- CP 1147, Baker FY 1990 budget request.
- CP 1245 and CP 1256, Baker FY 1991 budget statements.
- Dispatch FY 1992 budget leads.
- Dispatch FY 1993 budget leads.
- OMB, Baker/Eagleburger, and White House budget files.

Selection logic: NSR-16 is the strongest internal starting point. Public budget
statements should provide chronology and fallback text, not substitute for
internal OMB/State/White House records.

### 4. Intelligence Matters: Covert Action, Oversight, and the Intelligence Budget

Purpose: document intelligence organization and management where it intersects
with covert action procedures, congressional oversight, NSC review machinery,
and the intelligence community budget.

Candidate sources:

- NSD 79, "Approval and Review of Covert Action," January 19, 1993.
- Intelligence Authorization Act, Fiscal Year 1991, Public Law 102-88.
- NSR 29, "Intelligence Capabilities - 1992-2005," November 15, 1991.
- NSD 67, "Intelligence Capabilities - 1992-2005," March 30, 1992.
- Press Secretary Fitzwater statement on organizational changes in the
  intelligence community, April 1, 1992.
- Intelligence Authorization Acts for Fiscal Years 1992 and 1993.
- NSC Intelligence Programs, DCI/Community Management Staff, OMB, and
  White House congressional liaison files.

Selection logic: NSD 79 is the chapter anchor for covert action, NSC/DC review,
legal review, restricted notification, delayed notification, and OMB resource
responsibility. NSR 29/NSD 67 and the FY 1992-FY 1993 intelligence
authorization record provide the separate intelligence-budget and community
management lane.

### 5. Chief-of-Mission Authority, Overseas Staffing, and Security

Purpose: document the President's field-management doctrine and the mechanism
for controlling interagency staffing overseas.

Candidate sources:

- President Bush's July 12, 1990 Chief-of-Mission letter.
- NAID 470760858, related Chief-of-Mission authority file and September 14,
  1989 Armacost variant.
- Chief of Mission Authority and Overseas Staffing / NSDD 38 package embedded
  in the NSR-16 scan.
- M/MO or M/MP files on NSDD 38, Special Embassy Program, and staffing disputes.
- DS and S/S files on post security implementation.

Selection logic: this is a core chapter. The Bush letter and an implementation
document should both be pursued.

### 6. Foreign Service Personnel and Department Management

Purpose: cover personnel, diversity, skills, assignments, administrative
capacity, and Department modernization.

Candidate sources:

- Dispatch, "Foreign Service Preparing To Meet Challenges of Tomorrow," February
  18, 1991.
- CP 1245 "Conduct of Foreign Affairs" section.
- M, DG/HR, DS, and S/S implementation files.
- Late-1992 Department reorganization lead in Dispatch.

Selection logic: public material identifies themes, but internal management
files are needed for final selection.

### 7. Foreign Assistance Reform and Coordination

Purpose: show how the administration tried to make foreign assistance a more
flexible foreign-policy instrument.

Candidate sources:

- NSR-16 questions on foreign assistance and Executive Branch flexibility.
- CP 1277, Citizens Democracy Corps.
- CP 1289, assistance and reform for Eastern Europe and Central America.
- Dispatch 1991 International Cooperation Act / foreign-assistance-law reform
  items.
- Zoellick, AID, Baker, S/P, and congressional strategy files.

Selection logic: use this chapter only for management/coordination, not regional
aid policy as such.

### 8. New Independent States Assistance Machinery

Purpose: cover the large 1991-1992 management problem created by the Soviet
collapse.

Candidate sources:

- Dispatch, January 1992, coordinating assistance to the New Independent States.
- Dispatch, October-November 1992, FREEDOM Support Act and NIS assistance
  implementation.
- Zoellick, Kanter, AID, NSC economic directorate, Baker, and White House files.
- Central Foreign Policy File all-post and new-embassy guidance.

Selection logic: this is the strongest 1991-1992 addition found so far and
should be developed as a late-volume chapter if internal files support it.

### 9. Selective Interagency Case Studies

Purpose: include only cases that reveal machinery.

Candidate lanes:

- Refugee admissions and migration budget: CP 1163, CP 1307, Dispatch FY 1993
  MRA budget, NSC/DC refugee files.
- COCOM/export controls: CP 1290, NSR-22, NSD-39, NSD-53, NSC/DC files.
- Public diplomacy/USIA: NSR-16, Gulf public diplomacy NSC/DC lead, USIA/BIB
  files.
- International organizations/arrears: CP 1171, CP 1219, CP 1245, CP 1256,
  Dispatch FY 1992 IO budget request.

Selection logic: no case study should be included simply because the policy is
important. It must illuminate organization, management, coordination, budget,
or implementation.

## Working Inclusion Priority

1. Bush Chief-of-Mission letter, final all-post version.
2. NSR-16 plus response/options/follow-on records.
3. Chief-of-Mission/NSDD 38 staffing implementation document.
4. NSD 79 covert action approval/review/congressional notification directive.
5. NSR 29/NSD 67 intelligence capabilities and NFIP budget mechanism records.
6. NSC transition organization/process document.
7. Internal FY 1991-FY 1993 budget management document.
8. Internal foreign-assistance reform or NIS coordination document.
9. Internal Foreign Service/personnel/security implementation document.
10. One or two interagency case studies if they show machinery.

## Open Decisions

- Whether to give NSC process its own chapter or use it only as the opening
  institutional frame.
- Whether public diplomacy/USIA deserves a chapter or remains a budget/process
  subsection.
- Whether refugee admissions are central enough for a case study.
- Whether late-1992 Department reorganization belongs in this Bush volume or
  mainly anticipates the Clinton administration.
- Whether NSD 79, dated January 19, 1993, can be used as the Bush
  administration capstone document for covert-action management in a 1989-1992
  volume.
